Haixing Li is Assistant Professor in the Department of Physics at City University of Hong Kong. She obtained her B.S. in Physics from the University of Science and Technology of China in 2012, where her undergraduate thesis with Prof. Xianhui Chen grew oxides in search of superconductors; she also spent a summer at the University of Oxford learning quantum optics. She then moved to Columbia University and earned her Ph.D. in Applied Physics in 2017 under the guidance of Prof. Latha Venkataraman, uncovering the electronic properties of molecular silicon. From 2017 to 2021 she worked as a postdoctoral fellow and then a Charles H. Revson Senior Fellow in the laboratory of Prof. Ruben Gonzalez, studying the mechanisms of ribosomal frameshifting.

The Li Laboratory examines molecules and develops measurement tools at the single-molecule level to spark advances in electronics and sustainability. Using break-junction measurements together with *in situ* imaging, the group quantifies charge transport through single-molecule junctions, investigates how quantum interference can be used to switch a junction on and off, and explores how molecules and biomolecules can be wired to electrodes through robust chemical linkers.
